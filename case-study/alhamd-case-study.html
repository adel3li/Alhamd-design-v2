Before you read anything else

Spend eighty seconds without something.

This is the product’s core flow, with today’s welcome blessing, نعمة الإسلام. Five sentences on a still screen, one at a time, each held for a fixed length. Arabic is the original; the English line is a gloss for this page.

The method has a name in the app: استشعار القيمة, “sensing the value.” You don’t know what something is worth until you imagine losing it. Then you come back, find it still in your hands, and the thanks comes from the heart before the tongue.

    The problem○

    A blessing that lasts becomes a habit. And nobody thanks a habit.فالنعمة إذا دامت صارت عادة، والعادة لا تُشكر.

    Gratitude, الحمد, is the first word of the Qur’an after the opening invocation. It is supposed to be constant. In practice it gets said fifty times a day and felt almost never. Sight, water, a parent’s voice, the ability to stand up: these are exactly the things we stop noticing, because they never leave.

    The obvious product here is a dhikr counter with a streak. The category is full of them. They are good at getting people to open the app and bad at the thing the app is for. A streak teaches you to protect the number. When the number breaks, the guilt is about the number, and the most common next step is to stop.

    So I framed the job differently. Not “help people say thanks more often.” Help someone feel the weight of one blessing, once a day, and then let them go. Everything below is a decision that follows from that sentence, including several that cost engagement on purpose.

    Bet one1Count nothing

    I shipped a streak counter. Then I took it out.

    Version 1 had the standard kit: a consecutive-day counter and nine celebration milestones from 3 to 365 days. It did what streaks do: it gave people a number to protect. It also contradicted the one promise on the onboarding screen: لا نِقاط، ولا سلاسل، ولا مقارنة بأحد, “no points, no chains, no comparing yourself to anyone.”

    The argument that settled it came from the content, not a dashboard. The hadith the app quotes on that same screen says the deeds God loves most are the most consistent ones, even if small. Consistency is the goal; a counter that punishes one missed day works against it. And in worship, a number you can show people invites the wrong intention.

    Bet two2One blessing, everyone

    A community with no feed, no profiles and no server.

    I wanted people to feel they weren’t reflecting alone. The usual answer is a social layer: posts, likes, followers. That would bring back every comparison I had just removed, and it would need accounts.

    Instead, every device computes the same number for the same day and uses it to pick from the same ordered list of 40 blessings. Everyone opens the app to the same blessing, wherever they are. The sense of togetherness comes from the content, not from watching each other. The only exception is your first open, which is always نعمة الإسلام, the blessing all the others rest on.

    Bet three3Design the pause

    Eighty seconds is a design material.

    Every one of the 40 blessings is written to the same five-step script, and every script is exactly 80 seconds. The timing is the design. The two “absence” steps get the most time, because that is where the feeling happens. The return and the praise are shorter, because relief is fast.

    Then I specified the stillness as carefully as any screen:

    400 msEach sentence fades in. Slow enough to feel placed, fast enough not to be noticed.
    2.5 sThe background shifts between moods. 0.8 s with reduced motion, and the glow never pulses.
    33A heavy haptic on every 33rd tasbih, the traditional count. A light tap on each step.
    44 ptMinimum touch target. People reflect at night, half asleep, with one thumb.
    4.5:1Every text color is checked against its real background, including 11 px labels.
    0Hourly haptic reminders. It was in the spec. I cut it: a nudge every hour is noise, not remembrance.
    Bet four4Trust is the product

    One misquoted verse and nobody trusts the other thirty-nine.

    A gratitude app for Muslims lives on Qur’an, hadith and scholars’ words. Users will forgive a bug. They will not forgive a wrong vowel in a verse or a weak hadith presented as sound. So I treated sacred text like regulated data, with a review state on every field that can be wrong.

    Qur’anUthmani script, verified flagMatched against a trusted source before release.
    HadithSource, number, gradeThe grade is shown to users, and verified separately.
    ScholarsMarked as paraphraseNever presented as a word-for-word quote unless it is one.
    EverythingReviewer, date, statusNothing ships as “approved” without a named reviewer.

    This is the honest state today: all 40 blessings and all 28 journey stations are written and structured, and all are waiting for review. The app is built so that flipping a field is the only change needed. I would rather launch late than launch wrong.

    Bet five5Measure less

    Thirty-two events became five.

    Version 1 sent 32 kinds of event to a product analytics service. Some of them sat right next to people’s private reflections: journal opened, journal saved, personal blessing edited. Version 2 has no account and no login; everything a person writes stays on their device.

    Analytics are off until you say yes. Then only five events exist, and the code keeps an allow-list of five metadata keys. Anything else, including any text a user typed, is dropped before it is stored. Privacy is enforced in code, not promised in a policy.

    Business model◇

    Never charge for the prayer. Charge for the path.

    The daily blessing, the reflection, the journal, the calendar, the tasbih and Hisn al-Muslim are free, permanently. Putting worship behind a paywall would break trust with exactly the people who would pay.

    What people will pay for is depth they can’t get from one blessing a day. “My Journey” is a guided path of four stages and seven stations each, organised by theme, with a journaling prompt and one small real-world action at every station. The first two stations are free, so the paywall only appears after someone has felt the value. Two plans: annual and lifetime. There is deliberately no monthly plan that renews quietly.

    Design system◐Night & Day

    A night palette for reflecting. A day palette taken from the icon.

    The default is Layl, night: most reflection happens after Isha or before Fajr, and a dark ground lets one sentence carry the whole screen. Gold is the thread; violet appears once per screen, only on the action.

    Nahar, day, is not an inverted night. I derived it from the app icon’s clay and cream, and let one terracotta replace both the violet and the gold. Every muted text color was measured: the night grey passes 4.5:1, the day brown 5.6:1 on cream. There is no Latin typeface anywhere in the app.

    The mark is the word and a thread, one measured unit: thread 0.02 × type size, dot 0.075 ×, gap 0.20 ×. The dot sits flush with the alef on the right, where Arabic reading begins. In the reflection, the same thread becomes the progress bar. On this page it is the line under every chapter.

    What I won’t do if day-7 retention is low: bring back streaks. I’d test a gentler daily reminder time and a shorter first reflection first, and I’d read the drop-off by step, because the steps are fixed and comparable across all 40 blessings.

    Most products ask how to keep you.
    This one asks how to let you go, grateful.

    What I’d do differentlyGet a scholar on the team in week one, not after the content was written. Review is the critical path, and I found that late.
    Still decidingWhether the hadith of the day sits above or below the blessing, and whether the tasbih shows the session count only or a lifetime total, which starts to look like a score.
