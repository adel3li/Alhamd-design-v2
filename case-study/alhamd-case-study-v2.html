Product case study · Alhamd الحمد

Everything I built took out.

Alhamd is a daily gratitude ritual for Muslims. It got better every time I removed something: the streak, the account, the feed, 27 analytics events. This is the story of those cuts, what each one cost, and why I’d make them again.

20 of the app’s 40 blessings. You stopped noticing most of them years ago.

The problem

Gratitude said every day stops being felt. Habit apps fix how often you say it, not whether you mean it.

The bet

One blessing a day. Imagine it gone for 80 seconds, then get it back. Count nothing.

The cuts

Streaks, accounts, a social feed, 27 of 32 tracked events, and any paywall on the daily ritual.

The status

Built solo in nine weeks. Pre-launch: scholar review of the content is the last hard gate.Live. Built solo in nine weeks; the scorecard below is what I’m watching.

STEP 1 OF 5 · 10 Sالتهيئة والحضور

Said fifty times a day. Felt almost never.

الحمد لله, “praise be to God,” is the opening line of the Qur’an and the most repeated phrase in a Muslim’s day. Repetition is the problem. Sight, water, a mother’s voice: the blessings that never leave are the ones we stop seeing.

The app’s answer is a method it calls استشعار القيمة, sensing the value. You take one blessing, imagine your day without it, then return and find it still there. Five steps, 80 seconds, the same script for all 40 blessings.

This case study follows the same five steps. Try the real one first; the gold thread at the top of this page works the way the one on the phone does.

    STEP 2 OF 5 · 20 Sتخيّل الغياب

    I shipped a streak. Then I imagined the app without it.

    Version 1 had the feature every habit app has: a consecutive-day counter and nine celebration milestones, from 3 days to 365. It also had an onboarding screen that promised the opposite: لا نِقاط، ولا سلاسل، ولا مقارنة بأحد. No points, no chains, no comparing yourself to anyone.

    I applied the product’s own method to the product. Don’t read about it. Miss a day and watch both versions.

    Version 1 · streak (simplified)
    6
    أيام متتالية من المداومة

    Six days in a row. The number is the reward.

    Version 2 · Hijri calendar
    بلا عدّاد
    ولا أيّامٍ «فائتة»

    Six days you reflected. No number anywhere in the app.

    In version 1, one missed day turns six days of worship into a zero. The guilt is about the number, and the easiest way to stop feeling it is to stop opening the app. In version 2 an empty day is just empty. It is never red and never called missed.

    The deciding argument came from the content itself. The hadith on that onboarding screen says the deeds God loves most are the most consistent, even if small. And in worship, a number you can show someone invites the wrong intention.

    What it costThe streak is the cheapest day-7 retention there is, and I expect to pay for removing it. I chose a different measure of success, in step 5.

    STEP 4 OF 5 · 15 Sالعودة

    What was left was the product.

    Open your eyes: it’s still here. Take away the counter, the account and the feed, and four things remain. They are the whole product, and they are where the effort went.

    1 · A shared day, with no server

    Everyone gets the same blessing today.

    Every device turns today’s date into the same number and uses it to pick from the same list. Togetherness without profiles, posts or a backend. Your first day is always نعمة الإسلام.

    Everyone, todayنعمة الماء

    Computed in your browser with the app’s formula: pool[day % pool.length]

    2 · Eighty seconds, designed

    The pause is the interface.

    3 · Sacred text, treated like regulated data

    One misquoted verse and nobody trusts the other thirty-nine.

    Users forgive a bug. They don’t forgive a wrong vowel in a verse, or a weak hadith presented as sound. So every field that can be wrong carries its own review state: the verse, the hadith grade, and whether a scholar’s words are a quote or a paraphrase.

    If day-7 retention is lowI won’t bring the streak back. I’d first test the reminder time and a shorter first reflection, and read drop-off step by step, which works because all 40 scripts share the same five fixed steps.

    Most products ask how to keep you. This one asks how to let you go, grateful.

    What I’d do differently

    Bring a scholar in during week one. Content review turned out to be the critical path, and I found that after the content was written.

    Still deciding

    Whether the tasbih shows this session only or a lifetime total. A lifetime total starts to look like the score I removed.

    What this shows

    I can hold a product to one sentence, say no to the easy metric, name what each no costs, and still ship: seven milestones in nine weeks, alone.
